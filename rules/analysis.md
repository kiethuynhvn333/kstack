# Digital media analysis — rules 22–29

**Load when:** analyzing performance, comparing periods or channels, diagnosing a change, or
planning or reading an experiment.

## Rules
22. Connect recommendations to the user's business objective and the role of the channel. Do not
    assume that more traffic or cheaper platform conversions means better business performance.
23. Before using a metric, establish its definition, source, date range, timezone, currency,
    population, counting unit, attribution window, and denominator.
24. Check data freshness and completeness. Distinguish missing data, zero activity, delayed
    reporting, and modeled results.
25. Compare equivalent scopes and periods. Check tracking changes, seasonality, audience or campaign
    mix, and conversion delay before interpreting a difference.
26. Validate important calculations with raw examples, reconciliation, or another independent
    method. Repeating the same calculation is not independent verification.
27. Diagnose across measurement, delivery, audience, creative, destination, and the business offer.
    Test competing explanations; a before/after change alone does not prove causation.
28. For experiments, state the hypothesis, primary outcome, comparison method, budget, duration,
    guardrails, and decision rule. Record overlapping changes that weaken interpretation.
29. Verify changing platform capabilities against current official documentation or observed
    account behavior before relying on them.

## Where the answers come from
- Metric definitions and trusted sources (rule 23): `workspace.md` → Metrics, then `knowledge/metrics.md`.
- Past findings to check first (rule 17): the project's `mistakes-and-learnings.md` and
  `knowledge/channels/<platform>.md`.
- Raw-example and fan-out checks (rule 26): `skills/quick-metric-self-check/`.
- Experiment fields (rule 28): the columns of `experiments.md` — fill every one before launch.
